# Stress test

Used in step 1.2 and in the express proposal. The goal is to press the central idea and the storyline until they can stand up to the room: better for the deck to take the beating here than in the meeting.

## How to run it
- Use the question-round format from `SKILL.md`.
- **Open with the pre-mortem.** "The presentation is over and they said no, or nothing changed. Why?" Recommend the two or three most likely reasons for this deck and audience, each with how the storyline answers it. Imagining a failure that already happened surfaces risks that a plain critique misses.
- **Ground each question in this deck.** Find the weak spot in the material (a figure with no comparison, a claim with no source, a result with no control group) and recommend how to defend or fix it. A generic question gets a rubber-stamp answer.
- **Name the resistance.** For each objection, say whether it is logical (doubt about the evidence), emotional (values, threat, pride) or practical (cost, effort, risk to the person). Logical resistance is answered with evidence; emotional with respect for what the person values; practical by acknowledging the cost and sharing the burden.
- **Follow the tree.** The central idea first; only once it is firm, the other branches. Answers that open dependent questions go to the next round. Usually one to three rounds are enough; stop when every branch is resolved. In express mode, a single round.
- The bank below is a menu, not a script. Add the "Specific stress test" questions from the case file.

Every objection raised is resolved: it changes the storyline, becomes an objection beat or an appendix slide (and a likely question in `delivery.md`), or is left out on purpose and the user is told so. At the end, show what changed in the central idea and the storyline.

## Question bank by branch
- **Central idea:** If the deck had a single slide, what would it say? Could a reasonable person disagree? What does the audience gain or lose? Does it change any decision or action?
- **Audience:** What do they believe today, and what needs to change? Who in the room might feel threatened? What is the first question they will ask, and where is it answered?
- **Evidence:** Where does each number come from, and compared with what? What is the most fragile figure? Is any correlation presented as cause? Was anything that contradicts the idea left out, and why?
- **Attention:** Does the hook create tension tied to the central idea? Where is the memorable moment? On which slide will the room drift? Is anything there because it's interesting rather than because it serves the idea?
- **Cut:** What is the weakest slide, and what is lost without it? Is any slide there because it took work? Does the deck fit the time with room for questions? If the slot is halved, what goes first?
- **Close:** What should happen the following Monday because of this deck? Is the ask explicit — who does what, by when?
