# Case: procedure

A lesson to make learners use a tool or follow a process: onboarding on a system, filing an expense, setting up a campaign, running a safety check. The goal is that they **do it alone, correctly, the first time they really need to**.

## What the learner needs to leave with
- The task done once, by them, in conditions close to the real ones.
- The few steps where people go wrong, and how to notice it.
- Where to look when they forget a step.

## Diagnosis questions
- What is the real task, from start to end, and how often will they do it?
- Which steps do people get wrong, and what happens when they do?
- Will they have the tool open during the lesson? A practice environment?
- What changes often (screens, rules) and where does the current version live?
- Who do they ask when stuck? (Only the user knows; `[TO CONFIRM]` otherwise.)

## How to phrase the objective
The task + alone + the standard.
- Weak: "Training on the new expense system."
- Strong: "Files an expense report with receipts in the new system, alone, in under ten minutes, with nothing sent back."

## Recommended arcs
- **Task journey** (default): the steps in the order they will do them, each module one stage of the task.
- **Whole first, then parts**: show the finished task once, then teach it stage by stage, then do it whole again.
- **Mistakes first**, for a process people already do badly: start from the errors that cost the most.

## Practice that fits
Do the step right after seeing it, in the real tool or a copy; a full run at the end with a new case; a "spot the error" exercise on a filled-in example. Hinge questions ask what happens next or what is wrong in a screenshot.

## Facts to keep current
Screens, menus, limits and rules come from the tool's or organization's current documentation or the user, never from memory, with the date they were checked. A procedure that changed since the source was written is the commonest wrong fact in this kind of lesson.

## Sample module
The stage where people go wrong most often.

## Pitfalls
- Teaching every feature instead of the task.
- Demonstrating without the learners doing it, when practice happens in the lesson.
- Screenshots that will be outdated by next month without a date or source.
- No reference card or link to use afterwards.

## Specific stress test
- What will they do the first time they hit a case the lesson didn't show?
- Which step will they skip when no one is watching?
- Where is the reference they will use in a month, and does the lesson point to it?

## With practice afterwards
Fits when learners will have the tool afterwards: the lesson shows the task once, whole, then its stages; the challenge is the task on their own case, with a reference card of the steps and where to look when stuck.
