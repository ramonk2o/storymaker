# Case: workshop

A hands-on session that ends with something made: a first prototype, a plan, a map of a process, a set of prompts for their own work, a budget. The goal is that learners **leave with the thing made, and with the method to make the next one without help**.

## What the learner needs to leave with
- The thing itself, made on their own case.
- The method, in steps they can repeat.
- What to do with it next.

## Diagnosis questions
- What exactly do they leave with, and how good does it have to be?
- Do they bring their own case, or is there a prepared one? What if someone has none?
- Alone, in pairs or in groups? How many people, and how much help is available?
- What do they need ready beforehand (materials, access, a template)?
- What happens to the thing after the workshop?

## How to phrase the objective
The thing made + on whose case + the method.
- Weak: "Workshop on customer journey mapping."
- Strong: "Each team leaves with a map of one real customer journey of theirs, with the two worst moments marked, and can repeat the method with another journey alone."

## Recommended arcs
- **Show the result → method in rounds → build → share** (default): each round teaches one step of the method and makes that part of the thing.
- **Build first, then name the method**, for experienced groups.

## Practice that fits
Practice is most of the time, around two thirds: each module is a short explanation and a worked example followed by a building round with a clear output and a time box. Hinge questions become quick checks of each team's output before the next round. A short share at the end shows a few results and draws out what worked.

## The lesson file
Add to each module the round's instructions: what to make, with whom, for how long, and what a good output looks like. Add a "Before the workshop" section with what learners and whoever teaches must prepare.

## Sample module
The first building round, with its instructions and an example output.

## Pitfalls
- Too much explanation before the first round.
- Rounds with no clear output or time box.
- A prepared case so different from theirs that they can't transfer it.
- No time to finish; people leave with half the thing.

## Specific stress test
- What will a team with a weak case or no case do?
- Which round runs long, and what does whoever teaches cut?
- Will they use the method again within a month?
