# Case: concept

A lesson to make learners understand an idea well enough to use it: compound interest, how a vaccine works, what a language model is, supply and demand. The goal is that they can **explain it, predict with it or apply it to a case they haven't seen**; repeating a definition proves none of that.

## What the learner needs to leave with
- The idea in their own words, and one case where it explains something they see.
- The wrong intuition they came with, and why it fails.
- One case of their own life or work to which they can apply it.

## Diagnosis questions
- What should they be able to explain, predict or decide with this idea afterwards?
- What do they believe about it today that is wrong or incomplete?
- Which everyday situation does this idea explain for them?
- Which ideas does it rest on, and do they have them?
- Is there a common analogy for it, and where does that analogy break?

## How to phrase the objective
An action that uses the idea + the case + the standard.
- Weak: "Understand compound interest."
- Strong: "Given two loans, predict which one costs more over a year and say why, without a spreadsheet."

## Recommended arcs
- **Puzzle → idea → cases → apply** (default): open with something the wrong intuition can't explain, give the idea that does, try it on cases.
- **From simple to complex**, when the idea is built from smaller ones.
- **Two views**, when learners arrive with a strong wrong model: let them commit to a prediction first, then show what happens.

## Practice that fits
Predict-then-check, explain it to a partner, apply it to a new case, sort examples from non-examples. Hinge questions work especially well here, because misconceptions are specific.

## Sample module
The module that tackles the strongest wrong intuition.

## Pitfalls
- A definition first, with the example late or missing.
- An analogy taught without saying where it stops working.
- Checking with "does that make sense?" instead of a question that would reveal the mistake.
- History and trivia that don't help them use the idea.

## Specific stress test
- What will they predict wrongly before the lesson, and does a module make them see it?
- Could they apply this to a case not in the lesson?
- Which analogy will they keep, and will it mislead them later?
