# Stress test

Used in step 1.5 and in the light proposal. The goal is to press the map until it can survive real learners: better for the lesson to fail here than in the room.

## How to run it
- Use the question-round format from `SKILL.md`.
- **Open with the pre-mortem.** "The lesson happened, and a week later the learners can't [objective]. Why?" Recommend the two or three most likely reasons for this lesson and these learners, each with how the map answers it. Imagining a failure that already happened surfaces risks a plain critique misses. With practice afterwards: "A week later they haven't tried it, or tried and got stuck. Why?"
- **Ground each question in this map.** Find the weak spot (a module with no practice when practice happens in the lesson, a key idea that needs a term defined later, a time budget that only works if nothing goes wrong) and recommend how to fix it. A generic question gets a rubber-stamp answer.
- **Follow the tree.** The objective first; only once it is firm, the other branches. Usually one or two rounds are enough; in the light path, a single round.
- The bank below is a menu, not a script. Add the "Specific stress test" questions from the case file.

Every risk raised is resolved: it changes the map, becomes a note for whoever teaches, or is left on purpose and the user is told so.

## Question bank by branch
- **Objective:** Is it an action you could watch someone do? Does the final task prove it, or only that they listened? Is it theirs, or the organization's?
- **Learners:** What do they already know that the lesson repeats? What does it assume they know that they don't? Why would they care, in their own words?
- **Cut:** Which module is in because it matters to you, not to the learner? If the lesson lost a third of its time, what goes first? What can live in the support material?
- **Order:** Is every term defined before it is needed? Does each module need the one before it?
- **Practice:** Where do they first do something themselves, and how many minutes in? Does each hinge question's wrong answer reveal a real mistake? Is the final task like the real thing? With practice afterwards: could they start the challenge alone tomorrow, with only what the lesson gave them?
- **Facts:** Which claim would an expert in the room correct? Which fact is most likely to be outdated? Is anything taught as settled that sources dispute?
- **Time:** Which module runs long when the group asks questions? What does whoever teaches cut on the day?
