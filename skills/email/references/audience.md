# Audience

Used in the Opening and the Strategy step. Who receives the email changes what it can do, so the shape of the audience and the direction of power are settled before the move.

## Shape

- **One reader.** The email is written for one person's state, history and stakes. The risk is forwarding: write nothing that would hurt the user if someone else read it.
- **A few readers.** Each reads for "what does this mean for me", and they read each other reading. To and Cc are part of the message: To acts, Cc is informed, and copying someone's manager is itself a move, so name it in the card. Name in the body who does what. When the readers' stakes differ, consider separate emails, or a word with one of them first.
- **Many readers.** Nobody can ask a question before reacting. The email is read on a phone, skimmed, forwarded out of context and quoted. Write for the most suspicious reader; answer "what does this mean for me" in the first lines; leave out in-jokes and anything only part of the group knows; say where questions go, and how to reply without replying to everyone when that matters.

## Power and relationship

- **Up** (a manager, a client, anyone with power over the user): lead with what they need to decide; respect their time; disagree with facts and a proposal, not a complaint; preserve their face.
- **Across** (peers, partners): anchor in the shared goal; be explicit about who does what.
- **Down** (people the user leads or who depend on them): clear and kind. If the decision isn't open to debate, saying so is a form of respect; explain how it was decided.
- **Outside** (people who don't know the user): earn the first line; say why them and why now.

## Reader profile

For each reader who matters (up to three; in a many-reader email, up to three types), settle: what they already know, what they want, what they fear, what they are probably feeling now (read the thread), and what they gain or lose from this email. This profile goes to the cold reader, without the objective.

## Pitfalls

- Writing for the person in copy instead of the person in To.
- An email to many for something that needed one-to-one conversations first.
- Someone learning something important about themselves in a group email.
- Reply-all used to show the group who is right.
