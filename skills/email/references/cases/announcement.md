# Case: an announcement to many

A change told to a team, a department, all clients or a whole list: a reorganization, a new policy, a leadership change, cuts, a price change, a departure. Many readers, each reading for **what changes for them, why, and what they should do**, with no chance to ask before reacting.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- What changes, and from when.
- What it means for them, by group when groups are affected differently.
- Why, and how it was decided.
- What doesn't change.
- What to do now and where questions go.

## Materials that help
The decision and its date; who is affected and how; the reasons and how the decision was made; what was already communicated, to whom; the questions people will ask; any legal or HR wording that must be kept.

## Diagnosis questions
- Who is most affected, and have they heard it first, in person?
- Do different groups need different emails?
- What will the most skeptical reader suspect?
- What question will everyone ask, and does the email answer it?
- Is there wording that legal, HR or a client contract requires?

## How to phrase the message
What changes + from when + what it means for the reader.
- Weak: "Some organizational updates."
- Strong: "From March 1, the design and content teams become one studio led by Carla; nobody's role or pay changes, and your current projects keep their leads until they ship."

## Typical structures
- **What, why, what next:** the change and its date → what it means for each group → why and how it was decided → what stays the same → what to do now → where questions go.
- **Sensitive change:** the news → acknowledgment of what it means for people → the facts of support (dates, contacts, what is offered) → how it was decided → where to talk.

## Pitfalls
- People learning in a group email something about themselves they should have heard in person.
- Euphemisms ("rightsizing", "optimizing the structure") that the skeptical reader decodes as spin.
- Answering only the company's questions, not the reader's.
- No place for questions, which sends them to the hallway.
- A tone celebrating a change that costs part of the audience.

## Sample passage
The subject line and the first paragraph, which must work alone.
