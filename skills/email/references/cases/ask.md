# Case: a hard ask

Asking for something the reader may not want to give: a raise, budget, more time, an exception, an introduction, a favor; collecting what is owed (a payment, a delivery, an answer that is late); following up on something unanswered; a cover letter. The reader wants **to know exactly what is asked, why them, and what saying yes costs and gives them**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- The ask, specific: what, how much, by when.
- Why it is reasonable, in the reader's terms.
- How easy yes is: the next step, already prepared.

## Materials that help
What was agreed before and where; the numbers or results behind the ask; what the reader gains; previous messages that went unanswered; for a cover letter, the role and why the user fits it.

## Diagnosis questions
- What exactly is the ask, and what is the minimum the user would accept?
- What does the reader gain, or avoid, by saying yes?
- Was this agreed before? Where is it written?
- Is this the first ask or a follow-up? How long has it waited?
- What happens if the answer is no, and should the email say so?

## How to phrase the message
The ask + the reason that matters to the reader.
- Weak: "Following up on the invoice."
- Strong: "Invoice 214, due on May 5, is still open; can you confirm payment by Friday, or tell me who in finance should have it?"

## Typical structures
- **Direct ask:** the ask → the reason → what yes involves → the date.
- **Anchored ask:** the shared goal or the result delivered → the ask that serves it → the next step.
- **Follow-up:** the reference to the earlier message, quoted or linked → the ask again, simpler → an easy way to answer, including no.
- **Cover letter:** why this role and this company → the two or three proofs that the user fits → the ask for a conversation.

## Pitfalls
- The ask buried at the end of a long context.
- Apologizing for asking, which signals the user doesn't think it's fair.
- A follow-up that guilt-trips ("I know you're busy, but…").
- An ask with no date, which gets no answer.
- A cover letter that retells the resume instead of making the case.
