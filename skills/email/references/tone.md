# Tone and voice

Used in the Opening (voice), the Strategy step (tone) and the light path's draft. Tone is settled as a position on three axes plus the user's voice as traits, never as one adjective, because "friendly but firm" means different things to the user and to the model.

## Three axes

- **Proximity** — from close (first names, shared references, the way the two of them usually talk) to institutional (titles, full names, no shared shorthand). Set by the existing relationship and the thread, not by the subject.
- **Firmness** — from open (exploring, asking, willing to change) to non-negotiable (decided, with the reason and what happens next). Set by what is actually decided.
- **Warmth** — from cool (the facts, no feelings named) to warm (acknowledging what the reader is going through, appreciation). Set by the reader's situation and what the relationship needs.

The axes move independently. Close and non-negotiable: "Ana, I won't make Friday; here is what I can deliver and when." Institutional and warm: an announcement of cuts that treats people with respect. The most common mistake is lowering firmness to raise warmth, which leaves the reader unsure what was decided. Recommend a position on each axis with a one-line reason; in the draft, a sentence shows it.

## Matching the thread

Keep the thread's formality, length and greetings unless the move needs a change. A sudden change of register is itself a message: going formal reads as cold or as escalation. When the card changes the register on purpose, say so.

## Voice

The email goes out under the user's name, so the voice is theirs.

- Read one to three messages the user sent to similar readers, or their own messages in the thread, and describe what they actually do in four to six traits, each written as "does X, not Y": greeting and sign-off; sentence length; I or we; vocabulary and jargon; how directly they ask; two or three things they never do.
- Without samples, derive the traits from the thread and the relationship, label them as a guess, and ask for one message in one line.
- An adjustment the user asks for ("more formal than I usually am") moves a trait, not the whole voice.
- If the user wants to sound like someone else, take one or two traits as adjustments to their own voice; never imitate passages.

## Pitfalls

- Stock email phrases ("I hope this email finds you well", "per my last email", "just circling back") replacing the user's voice.
- Exclamation marks and emojis added as warmth by reflex, or removed where the user uses them with this reader.
- Warmth that reads as backing down from the decision.
