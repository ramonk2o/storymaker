# Writing the email

Used for the light path's draft, the openings that show each move and the writing after the gate. These are rules the model doesn't follow on its own; the voice traits override them when they ask for something else. Facts follow "Nothing invented" in `SKILL.md`.

## Subject line (email only)

- Says what the email is about and, when the reader must act, what and by when: "Decision needed by Friday: Q3 budget", "Project X: new delivery date".
- Not a teaser. Not a harsh verdict to be read in an inbox list: name the topic ("About your proposal"), never mislead about it.
- In a reply, keep the thread's subject unless the topic changed.

## First lines

- They carry the message: the news, the ask, the decision, or why the reader is getting this. A reader who stops there knows what matters.
- At most one line of greeting, when the voice and the relationship call for it; no warm-up ("I'm writing to…").

## Body

- One block per card line, in order, doing its job.
- Every claim with its specifics: dates, numbers, names, what happened. Vague words ("some issues", "soon") are where trust leaks.
- Short paragraphs; a list only for steps, options or items the reader will act on.
- Name who does what. A passive that hides responsibility ("mistakes were made") reads as evasion.
- Each sentence earns its place: the reader needs it to understand, decide or feel what the objective says. Justification that only protects the writer goes.

## Close

- The concrete ask or next step: who, what, by when. When no answer is needed, say so.
- Nothing after it that weakens it ("but no worries if not", "sorry again").
- The sign-off follows the voice.

## Length

What the objective needs: one paragraph or twenty. Check it against how it will be read: a phone shows the first three or four lines, so a long email needs a first paragraph that works alone and blocks that can be skimmed.

## Channels

- **Email:** subject line; greeting and sign-off by the voice; readable by someone who never saw the thread.
- **Slack, Teams and similar:** no subject, so the first line does its job; shorter paragraphs; mentions only for who must act; only the formatting the channel renders. A long message to a channel can put the gist first and the rest in its thread.
- **WhatsApp and text messages:** no subject; the first message carries the point; short blocks; one message, not a burst of fragments.
- **Letters, cover letters included:** the greeting and closing convention expects; no thread to lean on, so the context goes in the first paragraph.

## Habits to avoid

- "Not X, but Y" reversals, lists of three by reflex, rhetorical questions.
- Stacked hedges ("I just wanted to maybe check whether").
- Apologizing by reflex, or for writing at all.
- Passive-aggressive phrasing ("as previously mentioned", "per my last email").
- Bold, capitals or exclamation marks as emphasis in a hard email: they read as shouting.
- Frequent dashes.
