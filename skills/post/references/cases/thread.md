# Case: thread

A chain of posts on X, Threads or another network that links replies: an argument, a story, an explainer or a list told in steps. Most readers see the first post in a feed and decide there; each later post is another chance to leave. The reader wants **a first post that is worth something on its own and promises more, and a reason to read every next post**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- From the first post: the point, or a promise specific enough to be worth the next tap.
- One idea per post, each moving the thread forward.
- A last post that lands: the payoff, the consequence or what to do, not a summary.

## Materials that help
The text, notes or link the thread comes from; the numbers, screenshots or quotes behind each step; past threads of the user's that worked; whether the account is known to the readers or reaches strangers.

## Diagnosis questions
- Which network and account, and who sees it?
- Is there enough for a thread, or is it one post's worth? Cut to the posts each idea earns.
- Does it argue, tell a story or explain?
- Does the first post stand on its own if someone only sees it (and it gets quoted alone)?
- Is there a link? Where it goes (first post, last post, a reply) depends on the network; look it up.
- When it comes from a longer text: which parts survive, and which need the text around them?

## How to phrase the central idea
What the reader gets by the end, with the proof, in a line the first post can carry.
- Weak: "A thread about our pricing change."
- Strong: "We raised our price 40% and lost 6% of customers; the ones who stayed were the ones we wanted, and here is how we told them apart."

## Typical structures
- **Argument:** the claim with its stake → the evidence, one piece per post → the objection → what follows.
- **Story:** the result or the moment of tension first → what happened, in order → the turn → what it taught.
- **Explainer:** the question → the answer in one post → steps or examples, one per post → where to go next.
- **List:** the promise with its count → one item per post, each with its own proof → the item that ties them together last.
- Each post reads on its own as well as in sequence; the number of posts is what the content earns, not a target.

## Pitfalls
- A first post that only announces ("A thread 🧵") instead of saying something.
- Posts split mid-sentence to fit the limit.
- Padding to look long; two posts that say the same thing.
- A last post that summarizes or asks for follows instead of landing.
- Numbering or emojis by reflex, when the network or the voice doesn't use them.
