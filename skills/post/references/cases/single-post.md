# Case: single post or caption

One post on a social network that informs, argues or tells something: a lesson from work, a take, an explainer, a caption for a photo or video. It is read in a feed, between other posts, and most readers see only what comes before the "see more" cut, or the first line next to an image. The reader wants to know **from the first line whether it is worth stopping for, and to leave with one idea they can use or repeat**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- From the first line (or what fits before the cut): the point, or a reason to expand.
- One idea, with the author's own proof: what happened, a number, a case.
- What to do with it, when the post has a takeaway.

## Materials that help
The idea as the user would tell it; the case, number or screenshot behind it; the image or video a caption goes with; one or two of the user's past posts that worked, for their voice; the network and the account.

## Diagnosis questions
- Which network, which account, and who follows it?
- What happened, or what did you see, that made you want to post this?
- What is the one idea, and what of yours proves it?
- Does the post argue (people will push back) or inform?
- Is there a link, a mention or an image that goes with it?
- For a caption: what does the image already say, so the caption doesn't repeat it?

## How to phrase the central idea
The idea + the proof, in a line the first line can carry.
- Weak: "A post about leadership lessons."
- Strong: "I stopped holding daily standups with my remote team and our delivery didn't slip; written check-ins showed who was blocked earlier."

## Typical structures
- **Lesson:** the result or the turn first → what happened → why → what the reader can take.
- **Take:** the claim → the case behind it → the objection in one line → what follows.
- **Explainer:** the question the reader has → the answer in plain terms → one example.
- **Caption:** a line that adds what the image can't show (the context, the number, the why) → at most a few lines more.
- A long single post whose order matters (a long LinkedIn post, say) takes the script path, with each paragraph as a unit.

## Pitfalls
- A first line that warms up instead of saying something.
- A hook the post doesn't pay off, or one that withholds the point to force a click on "see more".
- Generic advice anyone could post, when the value is what only the author saw.
- A caption that describes the image.
- Hashtags, emojis or a call for comments added by reflex; engagement bait ("Agree?").
- One sentence per line by reflex, which reads as a template.
