# Case: carousel

A post of several slides the reader swipes through, on Instagram, LinkedIn or another network, with a caption beside it: an explainer, a list, a story or a take told slide by slide. The cover appears in the feed among images and decides whether anyone swipes. The reader wants **a cover that says what they get, slides they can read at a glance, and a last slide worth saving or sharing**.

The skill delivers the words of each slide, a short visual note per slide, the caption and alt text. It doesn't design or generate the images; the visual note tells whoever designs them what each slide needs.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- From the cover: what the carousel gives them, in a few words.
- One idea per slide, readable in a couple of seconds.
- A last slide that lands or is worth saving: the payoff, the summary they would keep, or what to do.
- From the caption: the context the slides leave out, without repeating them.

## Materials that help
The text or notes it comes from; the numbers and examples behind each slide; the account's past carousels, for voice and visual habits; the brand's visual guide and template, if any, so the visual notes fit them.

## Diagnosis questions
- Which network and account, and who follows it?
- Is there a template or visual standard the slides must fit? It sets how many words fit per slide.
- Does it explain, list, tell or argue?
- What does the reader save it for: to use later, to share, to remember a number?
- When it comes from a longer text: which parts survive as one slide each?

## How to phrase the central idea
What the reader gets, in a line the cover can carry in a few words.
- Weak: "Tips on CRM emails."
- Strong: "Five subject lines that doubled our open rate, and the one rule behind all of them."

## Typical structures
- **Explainer:** the question on the cover → the answer → one slide per step or example → a slide that ties it together.
- **List:** the promise with its count → one item per slide, each with its proof → the item worth saving last.
- **Story:** the moment of tension on the cover → what happened, slide by slide → the turn → what it taught.
- **Take:** the claim → the evidence, one piece per slide → the objection → what follows.
- **Script line:** each slide gets its words (a headline and, if needed, a short body) and a visual note: what appears (a number, a chart, a photo, a quote), what stands out, and how it connects to the next slide.
- **Caption:** the first line works as the post's own hook in the feed; the rest gives context, sources and any link the network allows there.
- Alt text per slide carries the slide's words and what the image shows.

## Pitfalls
- A cover with a label ("CRM tips") instead of a promise.
- Too many words per slide; paragraphs where a line fits.
- Slides that only make sense with the caption.
- A caption that repeats the slides.
- A last slide that is only "follow for more".
- Visual notes that describe decoration instead of what the slide must show.

## Sample passage
The recommended cover and the second slide, with their visual notes.
