# Voice

Used in the Opening (voice source), in the voice step and, for a short post, when writing the draft. A voice is settled as observable traits the user can check on real sentences, never as adjectives alone ("punchy", "light"), because adjectives mean different things to the user and to the model. For a short post, derive the traits from the request, the network and any post of the user's, and show them in the draft instead of listing them.

## Sources of a voice

The user can set the tone in any of these ways, alone or combined. Recommend one from the reader and the network, and accept whatever the user brings.

- **A register:** casual, technical, provocative, institutional. Derive the traits from the reader and the network.
- **The user's own voice:** read two or three posts they wrote and like, and describe what they actually do, not what they say they do.
- **Reference posts or texts:** by anyone, whose tone the user wants. Describe the traits they share; where they disagree, ask which one wins. They ground the traits; they are never a template to copy.
- **A brand's voice guide:** turn the guide's adjectives into traits and check them on the brand's best recent posts.
- **A named person to take inspiration from:** see below.
- **Combinations:** "my own voice, but drier, like X" means the user's posts set the base and the named person adjusts one or two traits; say which trait comes from where.

## Traits to settle

Four to seven traits, each written as "does X, not Y" so it can be checked on the sample. Pick the ones that make this voice different from a neutral post; skip the rest.

1. **Sentence length and line breaks** — for example, "short sentences, a line break only where a beat lands, never one sentence per line by default".
2. **Person and distance** — I, we, you or impersonal; whether the post talks to the reader.
3. **Vocabulary** — plain or technical, the jargon allowed, slang, contractions.
4. **Certainty and edge** — how strongly claims are stated; humor, irony.
5. **Platform habits** — emojis, hashtags, mentions, numbering of a thread: which ones, how often, or none.
6. **Never does** — two to four explicit bans.

## Taking inspiration from a named person

- Describe the person's writing as traits, stated as your reading ("my reading of X: …"), and mark the ones you are unsure of. Ask for a post or passage the user likes, because it grounds the traits in something both of you can see. If the person isn't someone whose writing you know, ask for samples instead of guessing.
- Imitate traits, never passages: new sentences that share the person's rhythm and choices, no reproduced or closely paraphrased text, no signature phrases or catchphrases.
- Don't reuse the sentences, images or structure of any post the user brought. Before the style check, compare the post with those passages yourself and rewrite anything that echoes them, since the style check never sees them.
- Never present the post as the person's, and never attribute to them a quote you can't source.

## Pitfalls
- Adjectives standing in for traits.
- The network's clichés ("Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in") slipping in as if they were the voice.
- The model's own habits slipping in as if they were part of the voice.
- A voice that works against what the reader needs. When they conflict, the reader wins.
