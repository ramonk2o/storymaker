# Script, hooks and reverse outline

Used in the script step, in the Opening when a text is being turned into a post, and after writing to compare the post with the approved script. The script is the post read from above: one line per post or slide, so the user can judge the order, the count and the gaps before the rest is written.

## The line

```
1 [hook] The unit's claim, up to about 12 words — proof or material
2 [role] … — proof — visual: … (carousel)
```

- **One unit per line:** a post of a thread, a slide of a carousel, a paragraph of a long single post.
- **Role:** `hook` (the first unit), `setup`, `proof`, `turn` (where the post changes direction), `objection`, `payoff` (the last unit). Most middle units are `proof` or `setup`.
- **A claim, not a topic.** "The 6% who left were all on the discount plan", not "Churn". A line that needs an "and" is probably two units, or one unit carrying too much.
- **Proof or material:** what the unit uses (a number with its comparison, a case, a screenshot, a quote with its source), or "argument" when it reasons from the unit before. A claim with nothing next to it is a gap: find the proof, mark it `[TO CONFIRM]`, or cut the line.
- **Visual note (carousel):** what the slide shows and what stands out, in a few words. Not decoration.

## Checks before showing it

- **Sequence test.** Read only the lines, in order: they tell the whole post. Between two lines you can say "so", "but" or "because"; where only "and also" fits, the order is a list, and a list is fine only when the post promised one.
- **Count test.** For each line, what is lost without it? A unit kept to make the thread look substantial, or because it took work to find, goes. The count is what the content earns.
- **Standalone test.** The first unit is worth something to someone who sees only it. Every unit makes sense if quoted or screenshotted alone, without depending on the one before for its grammar.
- **Payoff.** The last unit gives what the first promised and adds something the middle didn't. A last unit that summarizes or only asks for follows is rewritten.
- **Limits.** Each unit fits the network's limit, from the research, with room to spare.

## Hooks

Two or three versions of the first unit, written in full as the reader will see them, each from a different angle:
- **The claim** itself, stated plainly.
- **The number** with its comparison.
- **The scene** or moment of tension.
- **The reader's question**, when they already have it.

Recommend one, with the reason in one line. Each hook must be paid off by the script as it stands; a hook the script doesn't pay off is cut or the script changes, with the user's ok. No hook withholds the point only to force the next tap.

## Reverse outline

The same line format applied to an existing text, used in two places:

- **Turning a text into a post (Opening).** Write one line per paragraph or section of the source with what it actually claims. Then mark which lines survive as units, which merge, and which need context the format can't carry. Propose the central idea the post keeps; usually one idea of the text, not all of it.
- **Checking the written post (after writing).** The cold reader summarizes the finished post unit by unit without seeing the script. Compare its lines with the approved ones: a unit whose summary doesn't match its planned claim drifted. Fix the unit, not the script, unless the user approves changing the plan.
