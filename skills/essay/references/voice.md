# Voice from an author

Used in step 5, when the user names an author whose voice the text should take.

- **Research their writing.** If the session can search the web, read a few of their published texts, enough to see what they do in all of them. If it can't, work from what you know of them, say so, and ask the user for a passage they like.
- **Turn it into traits.** Four to six traits a reader can check on a passage: tone, sentence rhythm, how they argue, how they use examples. Give them as your reading of the author, and mark the ones you are unsure of. When the text's language isn't the author's, say how each trait carries over, and drop one that only works in theirs. A trait about examples sets their kind, not their content: the examples still come from the material or the user.
- **Imitate traits, never passages.** Write new sentences that make the same choices. No passage of theirs reproduced or closely paraphrased, and no signature phrase, catchphrase or recurring image, which turn the text into a caricature and aren't the user's to take.
