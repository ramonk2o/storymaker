# Case: personal essay

A text built on the author's own experience that reaches something true beyond it. The story is the vehicle; the idea is the destination. The reader wants **to recognize something of their own in someone else's experience**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- A scene or experience they can see.
- What it revealed, stated plainly enough to carry away.
- Why it matters beyond the author.

## Materials that help
The author telling the story as it happened, out loud or in notes, with the specific details (places, words said, objects); photos or messages that fix details; texts whose tone they want.

## Diagnosis questions
- What happened, told as you would tell a friend?
- Which moment do you still see clearly?
- What did you understand then, and what do you understand now?
- What is true here for people who didn't live it?
- What will you not tell, or which names will you change?
- Is anyone in the story going to read it?

## How to phrase the central idea
Experience + what it revealed + what holds beyond the author.
- Weak: "What I learned when my father got sick."
- Strong: "Caring for my father taught me that help is mostly showing up on boring days; we save our presence for crises and miss the long ordinary middle where it counts."

## Recommended arcs
- **Scene to idea** (default).
- **Braid**, when the past and the present illuminate each other.
- **The question that resolves**, when the experience raised a question more than it answered one.

## Typical skeleton
1. Opening: the scene, in the middle of the action
2. What was at stake then
3. What happened, with the details that matter
4. The turn: what the author understood
5. Widening: what is true beyond the author
6. Close: back to the scene, or to now, changed

## Specific stress test
- Is every detail one the author gave you, or did the writing fill something in?
- Does the essay reach beyond the author, or does it stay a diary entry?
- Could anyone in the story be hurt or exposed in a way the author hasn't weighed?

## Pitfalls
- Invented or embellished details: in a personal essay this is the worst failure, because the author signs it as true.
- Explaining the feeling instead of showing the scene.
- A lesson stated as a moral at the end.
- Too much backstory before the scene.

## Sample passage for the plan
The opening scene, written with the author's real details; it shows the voice and whether the scene carries the idea.
