# Case: reflective essay

A text that thinks through a question in public: what something means, why it is the way it is, what to make of it. Its value is the path of the thinking as much as where it lands. The reader wants **to see the question differently by the end**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- The question, and why it matters to them.
- A way of seeing it they didn't have before.
- Where the author lands, with the honest uncertainty that remains.

## Materials that help
Notes, fragments, quotes the author keeps returning to (with sources), conversations or scenes that started the question, texts whose tone the author wants.

## Diagnosis questions
- What is the question, in one sentence?
- Why does it bother you now?
- What are the usual answers, and why don't they satisfy you?
- Where do you land, even provisionally?
- What do you still not know, and are you willing to say so in the text?
- What scene, object or example makes the question concrete?

## How to phrase the central idea
Question + where the author lands + what it changes. A reflective essay still lands; one that only wanders is notes.
- Weak: "Some thoughts on boredom."
- Strong: "We treat boredom as a problem to fix with the phone, but it was the only time the mind wandered toward what it didn't know it wanted; protecting a little of it is worth the discomfort."

## Recommended arcs
- **The question that resolves** (default).
- **Scene to idea**, when one moment carries the question.
- **Braid**, for longer pieces with a story running beside the thinking.

## Typical skeleton
1. Opening: the question made concrete in a scene or object
2. Why it matters
3. The usual answers, and where each falls short
4. The turn: the way of seeing it that the essay proposes
5. What it explains that the usual answers didn't
6. What remains uncertain
7. Close: back to the opening, seen differently

## Specific stress test
- Does the essay land somewhere, or does it end on "it's complicated"?
- Is each step of the thinking on the page, or does the turn come from nowhere?
- Would a reader who never had the question still care?

## Pitfalls
- Abstraction with no scene or example to hold it.
- Quotes standing in for the author's own thinking.
- A turn that isn't earned by the paragraphs before it.
- Hedging every sentence instead of being uncertain once, where it matters.

## Sample passage for the plan
The opening, where the question becomes concrete; when the risk is the turn, the turn's paragraph.
