# Case: blog post or newsletter issue

A post for a blog, or an issue of a newsletter, read by someone who chose to click or open it and can leave at any line. The reader wants to know **quickly whether it is worth reading, and to leave with something they can use or repeat**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- From the title and the first paragraph: whether the post is for them.
- One idea or technique they can use or repeat.
- Proof that it's real: what happened, with the author's specifics.

## Materials that help
The notes or thread the post comes from; numbers, screenshots, links; a past post or issue the author liked, or one by someone whose tone they want; the platform's or company's guidelines, if any.

## Diagnosis questions
- Who exactly is this for, and what are they trying to do when they find it?
- What should they be able to do or decide after reading?
- Why write it now: a question you keep getting, something that changed, a mistake you keep seeing?
- What is the one example, number or story of yours that proves it?
- Where does it run, and is there a length or voice guide?
- Is there a call to action? If the post's main job is sign-ups or sales, it is copy and out of scope.

## How to phrase the central idea
Takeaway + proof.
- Weak: "Some lessons from our database migration."
- Strong: "Our cutover took three weekends instead of one because we tested without production traffic; test under real load before you book the date."

## Recommended arcs
- **Lesson learned**, **How-to** or **Thesis first** (a take), by what the post does.
- **Newsletter issue:** one main item in one of those arcs, then short items. Its subject line and preview text are its title and opening; plan both. A recurring issue reuses the last issue's style sheet and structure, and takes the light path.

## Specific stress test
- Could anyone have written this, or does it carry what only the author saw?
- Does the title promise exactly what the post delivers?
- Where would a busy reader stop?

## Pitfalls
- A label title, or one that promises more than the post delivers.
- A preamble before the point.
- Generic advice instead of the author's specifics.
- Bullet lists in place of the story of what happened.
- Headings in a post under about 800 words.

## Sample passage for the plan
The title and the first paragraph, where the reader decides to stay. For a newsletter, the subject line, the preview text and the first paragraph.
