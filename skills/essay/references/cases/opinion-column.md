# Case: opinion column

An opinion column or op-ed: an argument on a subject, signed by the author, for an outlet, a company's channel or the author's own, within a word count and often edited by someone else. The reader wants to know **what the author thinks, why, and why it matters to them now**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- The author's position, in one sentence they could repeat or argue with.
- The reason that moves them most, with its evidence.
- Why it matters to them, and why now.
- The strongest objection, and why the author still holds the position.

## Materials that help
The notes or draft; the sources the argument rests on; the outlet's word count and guidelines; past columns of the author's, or of someone whose tone they want.

## Diagnosis questions
- What do you believe about this that most of your readers don't?
- What happened, or what did you see, that made you think so?
- What would a smart reader who disagrees say, and what do you answer?
- Is there a news hook that makes it timely? Without one, what makes it worth reading this week?
- Where does it run, with what word count, and who edits it?
- Why are you the one writing it: the line under the column that says who you are?

## How to phrase the central idea
Position + reason + what follows.
- Weak: "Remote work has pros and cons."
- Strong: "Remote work costs juniors most, because they lose the overheard conversations that used to train them; teams that go remote have to schedule what the office did by chance."

## Recommended arcs
- **Thesis first** (default): hook → position early → main reason with its case → objection and answer → what follows. Up to about 900 words, no headings.
- **Scene to idea**, for a longer column that opens with a case and returns to it.

## Specific stress test
- Why now, and why this author? Editors turn down columns that answer neither.
- Does the column fit the word count, or will the editor cut where the author wouldn't?

## Pitfalls
- No position, or the position in the last paragraph.
- A straw objection, or none.
- Research crowding out the author.
- A close that adds nothing the middle didn't give.

## Sample passage for the plan
The opening, 100 to 200 words: it shows the voice and how fast the position arrives.
