# Research and outlet rules

Used before the Essay outline when the text needs published facts beyond the material, or depends on an outlet's rules.

## Research when the material isn't enough
When the argument needs more than the material and the user can give (a claim that needs evidence, context the reader won't have, a counterargument no one raised), research published sources before the outline, if the session can search, and say in one line what you are looking for. Research covers published facts and sources, never what only the user can know (their experience, internal decisions). What you find counts as material: it keeps its source and appears in the outline with it. It supports or tests the user's view and never replaces it: if a finding contradicts the user or the material, show both in the next round and let the user decide. In the light path, research only what a pending item needs. If the session can't search, say what is missing and where it might be.

## Outlet rules
Word counts, editorial guidelines, how an outlet handles links, headings and bios change and differ by outlet. Look them up on the outlet's own page when the session can, with its date, or ask, or leave them `[TO CONFIRM]`.
