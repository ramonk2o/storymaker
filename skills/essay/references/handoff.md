# Handoff

Used to bring the delivery options with the results of the checks (Phase 3) and to deliver. The text is finished; what is left is getting it where it will be read in a form the user can still edit.

## Choosing how to deliver
1. **Look at what this session can do right now** to deliver a text: editable document artifacts, skills that build document files, connectors to apps where the text will live (a document editor, a blogging platform, a newsletter tool), and plain text in the reply. Offer only what is actually available; never assume a tool exists.
2. **Judge each option against this text,** from what the Essay outline says about the occasion: where it will be published, who edits it afterwards, and whether headings, links and footnotes have to survive the trip.
3. **Present two to four viable options,** numbered, each with one pro and one con for this text, and recommend one with the reason. In the light path, one line per option. The text in the reply, ready to copy, always exists as a fallback; if it is the only option, deliver it without listing options.

## Delivering
- The Essay outline is the specification: moving the text to another format doesn't reword it.
- Outside the text, deliver in a few lines: what changed from the outline, if anything; open items marked `[TO CONFIRM]`; and the result of each check that applies, one line each, or "not run". Keep the conversation and the delivery notes out of the text.
- The skill never publishes or schedules a text: publishing is the user's act. Save it as a draft when the session can.
- Don't move a text that still holds `[TO CONFIRM]` markers without saying so in the same line.
- A column goes with a one- or two-line bio of the author, from what the user said, and, when it goes to an outlet, an offer in one line to draft the pitch to the editor.
- A newsletter issue goes with its subject line and preview text.
- After moving the text, check that headings, links, footnotes and paragraph breaks survived.
- Offer, in one line, to keep the approved outline next to the text: it is the quickest way to rework the piece later.
