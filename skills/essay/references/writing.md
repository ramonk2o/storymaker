# Writing the text

Used in steps 2.4 (sample passage) and in Phase 3. These are rules the model doesn't follow on its own; they hold for every kind of text, and the style sheet overrides them when it asks for something else. Facts follow "Nothing invented" in `SKILL.md`.

## The outline is the map
- One paragraph per outline line, in order, making that line's claim with the proof listed next to it.
- A paragraph that wants to say something its line doesn't is a sign the outline needs to change: stop and ask, don't let the text drift.
- Transitions carry the logic the sequence test found ("so", "but", "because"), said in the text's own words, not as connectives bolted on.

## Opening
- The first lines do the job the arc gave them. No warm-up, no restating the subject, no "in this essay I will".

## Paragraphs and claims
- Every abstract claim gets a specific detail from the outline or the material: a number, a case, a name, a scene. If there is none, cut the claim or ask; never supply an experience, scene or result the author didn't give you.
- Certainty matches the evidence: state what is supported, mark what is inferred, and don't use precise numbers you can't back.
- One term per concept; don't vary words for elegance when the reader could take them for different things.

## Habits to avoid
These mark a text as written on autopilot:
- "Not X, but Y" reversals.
- Lists of three by reflex.
- A rhetorical question as a transition.
- An ending that restates the text, or "in conclusion".
- Stacked hedges and intensifiers.
- Frequent dashes.
- Bullet lists where the text should argue.

## Format
- Headings, subtitle and citations follow the case file and the Essay outline.
- Links and footnotes only where the outline says sources are cited that way.

## Length
Stay within about 10% of the length in the outline. If the content needs more, ask; an outlet's word limit wins.
