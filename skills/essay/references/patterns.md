# AI-typical patterns

Used in steps 5 and 6, on the voice sample and the text, not on the outline. These structures make prose read as machine-written when they are the default or repeat. Each is described by its structure, not its words, so it holds in any language; the examples only illustrate.

## Dosed

Each has a legitimate use. All six share one budget: 1 use per 500 words (rounded down, at least 1), at most 1 per arc part, never in consecutive paragraphs. Spend a use only where it is the best choice for that passage.

- **Correction:** denies a claim nobody made, then states the real one ("It's not X, it's Y"; "not only X but also Y"; "more than X, it's Y").
- **Threes:** items grouped in threes for rhythm, not because there are three.
- **Announcement:** says a point is coming instead of making it ("The truth is…").
- **Stacked fragments:** fragments piled up for emphasis ("Fast. Cheap. Wrong.").
- **Punchy closer:** a short sentence that ends a paragraph by restating it.
- **Delaying bridge:** a generic opener or empty transition before the subject ("When it comes to…", "That said,").

## Avoid

Never used on your own initiative.

- **Dash asides:** a clause set off by dashes where a comma, colon or period would do.
- **Instant answer:** a rhetorical question answered right away.
- **Uniform rhythm:** sentences or paragraphs of the same length and shape.
- **Summing-up ending:** a conclusion that sums up or restates the opening.
- **Balance without position:** weighs both sides and never chooses.
- **Generic claim:** vague attribution ("experts say"), a hollow metaphor or aphorism, a sentence that would fit any topic.
- **Forced intimacy:** treats the reader as a friend or conjures a shared scene ("Think of that colleague who…").
- **Pleasing emphasis:** ad-copy tone, praising the reader, intensifiers that sell ("it matters, and it matters a lot").
- **Hedge and certainty mixed:** heavy hedging beside constant flat certainty.
- **Formatting inside prose:** lists, bold, headings, emoji.

## What the user brings

What comes from the user passes in both groups and uses no budget: their sentences kept from a draft, their explicit request, a trait the approved sample names.

## Using it

- **Voice sample (step 5):** write it without the avoid patterns and within the budget. A voice trait that relies on one of these patterns is named among the traits, so the user approves or cuts it at the gate; any other trait describes choices, not patterns.
- **Reread (step 6):** count the dosed uses against the budget and look for the avoid patterns. Rewrite what exceeds the budget or appears, except what the user brought. An instruction to avoid a pattern doesn't always hold, so the reread is the check.
